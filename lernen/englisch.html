1 Migration to the EU

Erst selbst laut definieren, dann aufklappen und vergleichen.

EU citizen
A person who has the nationality of an EU member state. EU citizens have freedom of movement: they can live, work and study in any EU country without a visa or work permit.
DE: EU-Bürger/in
Red-White-Red Card
An Austrian residence and work permit for qualified workers from non-EU countries (third-country nationals). It uses a points system: qualifications, work experience, age and language skills (German/English).
Groups: very highly qualified workers, skilled workers in shortage occupations (skills shortage), other key workers, graduates of Austrian universities, self-employed key workers, start-up founders.
Valid for 2 years, then Red-White-Red Card plus (free access to the job market).
DE: Rot-Weiß-Rot-Karte
temporary residence
Permission to live in a country for a limited time and a specific purpose (studying, seasonal work, being posted by a company). You may not settle permanently.
Opposite: permanent residency.
DE: befristeter Aufenthalt
fixed (fixed-term) visa
A visa that is valid only for a fixed period and purpose, e.g. a working holiday, seasonal work or fixed-term employment. When it expires you must leave or apply again.
Schengen: Visa C = short stay (max. 90 days) · Visa D = longer stay (up to 6 months).
to immigrate / immigrant / immigration
To come into a foreign country to live there permanently. An immigrant is a person who does this.
DE: einwandern / Einwanderer / Einwanderung
to emigrate / emigrant / emigration
To leave your own country to live in another one. An emigrant is a person who does this.
DE: auswandern / Auswanderer / Auswanderung
Merktrick: Immigrate = In · Emigrate = Exit.
He emigrated from Austria and immigrated to Canada.

Auch wichtig

migrant
Anyone who moves from one place/country to another, for whatever reason (IOM definition).
refugee
Someone who has to flee their country because of war, persecution or disaster.
asylum seeker
Someone who has applied for protection (asylum) in another country and is waiting for the decision.
net immigration
Number of immigrants minus number of emigrants.
skills shortage
There are not enough qualified workers in a job area (→ that's why the Red-White-Red Card exists).
to be eligible for sth.
To have the right to get or do something, because you fulfil the criteria. Graduates are eligible for the RWR Card.

2 Living and working in Austria

Pros

  • high standard of living, safe country
  • good social security and health insurance
  • strong unions, good working conditions
  • perks: 13th and 14th salary (bonus), 5 weeks holiday
  • stunning nature: mountains, lakes, lush meadows, skiing
  • laid-back lifestyle, long lunch breaks, coffee houses
  • bang in the middle of Europe → easy to travel

Cons

  • lots of bureaucracy / paperwork
  • German + dialects are hard for foreigners
  • shops closed on Sundays (annoyingly)
  • high taxes, expensive flats in cities
  • foreign qualifications not always recognised (recognition)
  • people can seem grumpy or smug; hard to make friends
  • some jobs are precarious; freelance = less security

So formulierst du es

  • On the one hand, … On the other hand, …
  • One major advantage / drawback is that …
  • Admittedly, the paperwork is exhausting, but …
  • Austria has great nature, whereas / whilst the bureaucracy is annoying.
  • All in all, I think the pros outweigh the cons.
Beispielsatz: One major advantage of living in Austria is the high standard of living. Admittedly, the paperwork can be exhausting, but the social security system is very good.

3 The new slave trade

Slavery = people are treated as property; they can't withdraw and work without pay. It is illegal under international law, but still widely practised.

Push factors

drücken Menschen aus dem Heimatland

  • insufficient economic opportunities (no jobs, no prospects)
  • poor governance, corruption
  • conflicts, war, civil strife
  • lack of social security, disasters

Pull factors

ziehen Menschen ins Zielland

  • better pay and job prospects
  • large diaspora that helps them settle in
  • safety, peace, democracy
  • education, health care

Smuggling vs. trafficking

people smugglinghuman trafficking
What?bringing people across a border illegally (in an irregular fashion) for moneyrecruiting/moving people with force, threats or lies to exploit them
Consent?migrant usually agreesno real consent
Ends?at arrivalexploitation continues
Personsmugglertrafficker

Effects of people smuggling

on migrantsharm to physical & mental well-being, dangerous journeys (many die), illegal → no rights, debt bondage, forced labour
on host countriesbad reputation of migration, loss of trust, loss of control over borders
on criminal networksthey make billions of dollars

Forced labour

Work people do involuntarily, under threat of punishment. Typical: documents seized, wages kept, intimidation, violence, debt bondage (a “debt” you can never pay back).

Case studies (p. 146)

Food supplier
Lithuanian workers caught chickens; the eggs went to big UK retailers and restaurants. Appalling conditions, threats, mattresses full of insects, debt bondage. Called “one of the worst cases of exploitation ever uncovered in the food supply chain”.
Forced labour – 4 Czech men
Worked in a bread factory. The gangmaster seized their documents and all wages; one man was chained to a radiator, beaten, burned. They escaped when one feigned illness in the van and all ran away.
Ben
Unemployed, homeless, socially isolated British man. A couple offered work + accommodation; he had to pave driveways for little or no pay, suffered intimidation and violence and was terrified to leave.
